Skybox: stars, the Milky Way and how bright they may be CANDIDATE

A sky behind the system view, to give a sense of scale: the cluster is a small part of a galaxy. It is one sky for the whole cluster, generated from a seed and not tied to any system, so it does not reveal the cluster's flattened layout. It is drawn at infinity: it turns with the camera's heading and tilt, and does not move when the view pans or zooms. Stars are soft points, brighter ones larger, with counts that climb steeply towards the faint end. The Milky Way is a denser band of faint stars, tilted about 60° off the orbital plane. Stars are neutral grey-blue except in the pale-colour version of B and in C, which shows the other end of the range. Sol stands in for any system.

Standard orientation: north up, tilt 35°, at the system fit

Today · no sky

Rings, belt and markers on the plain ground. Nothing says the system is anywhere in particular.

A · Stars only

Soft points with a spread of brightness, and a denser faint population along the band. The band shows only as density.

B · Stars and a faint band

As A, plus a very faint glow along the band with a few dark lanes. The glow adds about 15 levels over the ground at its brightest, so rings and markers keep their weight.

B + pale star colours

B with the stars' natural colours, blue-white to orange, pulled 60% of the way back to neutral. At these brightnesses the tints read as a faint warmth and coolness, not as an orange or a blue on the map. This is the proposal for star colour.

C · Photographic style

Bright glow, a bright core, coloured stars and strong lanes. It is striking, but the sky becomes the loudest thing on screen, and the orbit rings and faint markers lose their weight.
