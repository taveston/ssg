Reference frame cue, exposure ring cues and label halo CANDIDATE

Two candidates for the system view, drawn in Sol. First, a dotted crosshair in the orbital plane, centred on the frame body: it keeps north and the plane visible when the orbit rings have faded. It comes flat (A) or with a vertical axis (B). Second, a direction chevron and a period line for exposure rings. Third, a halo behind labels, so orbit rings stop running through the text. Violet is a placeholder for a new "reference" colour, free of the affiliation colours and the warning orange. The north dial and scale bar are as built (MRK-16, MRK-17).

Today

Orbits give the plane, but nothing marks north or the frame itself.

A · In-plane crosshair

Two dotted axes through the frame body, running to the map edge and fading. The north arm is brighter and ends in an N. Ticks sit at the scale bar's distance and follow the plane's foreshortening. A gap round the marker keeps it from reading as a target reticle. Labels carry the halo from the last board, so the dots break where an axis crosses a name.

B · With a vertical axis

As A, plus a solid axis along the plane's normal: a longer half up with a small arrowhead, a shorter half down. Solid, so it never reads as a continuation of the dotted north arm, which lies on the same screen line at heading 0. It foreshortens with tilt.

Close in: Callisto's frame, heading turned 40° anticlockwise

Today

Every orbit ring has faded (MRK-14). A disc and an edge tab are all there is: no north, no plane.

A · In-plane crosshair

The axes pass behind the disc. The frame reads as a place in a plane, with north marked, and the dial agrees with the arm.

B · With a vertical axis

The upper half leaves the disc at its pole and runs over it. The lower half goes behind the disc and comes out beneath.

B at four tilts (heading 20°)

The vertical axis is the tilt gauge. Straight down it points at the camera and vanishes, so B becomes A. Near the horizon it is at its longest. The tilt limit is 85° (CAM-01).

Proposed · chevron and period

A fast orbit reads as a thicker ring. The proposal adds an open chevron on the ring, just ahead of the identity mark, pointing the way the object circles (anticlockwise from above here), and the period as the label's second line (UNIT-02). The chevron is fixed to the mark, so it implies no position beyond the one the mark already holds. A label moves to the other side of its mark when the chevron would run under it, as Europa's does. Both panels carry the 2 px halo.
