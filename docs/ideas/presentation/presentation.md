# Orphan Worlds: Presentation & Visual Style

**In short:** the game has three nested spatial layers (sector, system, planet) and a separate 2D administration view. The sector and system views are a vector plotting display at true scale, with fixed-size markers; combat uses the same display on its own clock. Any object can be inspected in a semi-photoreal inset whose fidelity follows what the faction knows. The camera is a map camera with limited tilt; selection (what you read about) and focus (what the camera tracks) are separate. Much of section 2 is now specced in `docs/spec/system-view.md`; this doc keeps the reasoning and what isn't specced yet.

*Status:* draft. Companion to the overview; status tags as there (**Decided**, **Direction**, **Candidate**). Reference sketches: [Inspect & Planetary Transition](https://claude.ai/artifact/E8U8j7d2HdB5gGd1S1W5WR), a design canvas with the system view, inspect in place, transition storyboard, planetary view and inspection fidelity states. *Reference Sketches* describes it in words.

---

## 1. View layers (decided)

| Layer | Purpose | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Sector | Gate network, systems, movement between systems | Vector display (section 2) |
| System | Bodies, orbits, ships, stations, gates within one system | Vector display (section 2) |
| Planetary | Settlement administration and ground combat on one body | See section 3 |
| Faction administration | High-level management, collated reports, character interactions | Fully 2D UI (direction; section 4) |

The first three are spatial layers, nested from sector down to planet. Faction administration runs alongside them; it isn't a level of zoom.

## 2. Spatial display

- **The problem (context):** at true scale, planets are far smaller than a pixel in a whole-system view, and ships are invisible at any zoom that shows more than one of them. Engagements span thousands of kilometres, so a close-up of one ship tells the player little.
- **Strategic display (decided):** the system and sector views are a vector plotting display. Orbits are faint lines, bodies and ships are solid markers, trajectories are lines. System, sector and combat views share one visual language; combat is the same display on its own clock (*Combat, Raiders & Diplomacy*). How combat is shown, from the map to the replay and the report, is proposed in *Combat View* (`combat-view.md`, candidate). It reads as the government's own instruments, which suits a faction's leader rather than a pilot.
- **Scale (direction):** positions and orbits are drawn at true scale. Markers are a fixed screen size by class, never physical size. Only the marker is stylised, never the geometry. Zoomed in close enough, a body is drawn as its true disc instead. (Specced for the system view: SCL-02, MRK-03, MRK-04, MRK-07.)
  - Marker shape and weight show the type: planet, moon, station, gate, ship, salvage site.
  - **Bodies are outlines, not areas (decided 2026-10-13, from F-125).** Mid-grey filled discs read as buttons or stale data and outweighed the contacts. Markers are now a hairline circle with an opaque inside: a centre dot for planets, a plain circle for moons, radial ticks for the star. The filled disc is kept for true-size discs close in, so crossing that zoom reads as "marker" becoming "object". (Specced: MRK-03, MRK-04, MRK-11, MRK-13.)
  - **Orbits too large to read as rings fade out (decided 2026-10-14, from F-122 and F-137).** Close in, a large orbit crosses the view as an unexplained straight line. First tried: draw the frame body's own orbit only as a fixed-length tail. In play that looked wrong: the tail's share of the orbit changed with zoom, a tail that changes length without a change of rate feels wrong in motion, and it only covered the frame body, so a planet's orbit came back in full when a moon was focused. Now any orbit fades out once its ring is far larger than the view, whatever the frame. The orientation cue comes from an edge tab for the frame body's parent, shown while the parent is off screen, styled like the gate tabs but with the parent's body marker beside the chevron. (A bearing tick on the outermost moon orbit was tried first, but it implied a link with that moon.) (Specced: MRK-14, MRK-15.)
  - **Plotting-instrument cues (decided 2026-10-13, from F-121).** A scale bar and a north dial make the display read as an instrument, and true scale is its premise. The dial points where north lies on the map, projected through heading and tilt, but stays flat and upright: a foreshortened dial would show tilt the orbit ellipses already show, and turns to a sliver near the tilt limit. (Specced: MRK-16, MRK-17.) **Candidate:** range rings, dashed, which suit the plotting look. A plain distance ring mostly repeats the scale bar, so a ring should show a capability: sensor range, weapon range, or how far a ship can get in a set time. It would centre on the selected own ship or station, and probably take the affiliation colour of whatever it describes. Revisit when sensors or transit are designed. *Combat View* 3 uses them in the replay for point-defence, radar and lidar range.
  - **A frame cue shows which body the view is centred on (decided 2026-10-16).** A dotted crosshair in the orbital plane runs through the frame body, in a colour used for nothing else. It gives the "system north" and the reference frame at a glance, and the violet adds some interest to a scene of terrain alone. Ticks at the scale bar's multiples sit on the arms instead of range circles, which would compete with the orbit rings and repeat the scale bar. A dotted vertical axis stands on the plane, shorter as the camera looks down, and is a small nod to the physical space behind the abstraction. Mock-up 19 compared the flat cross with the axis; the axis won. Mock-up 19 drew it solid, but in the build a solid 100 px axis looked stubby and out of place beside the dotted arms. It is now dotted too, and its length is a share of the map's height, so it fills most of the view at any window size. (Revised 2026-10-16.) Capability rings stay a candidate (above). The sector view may later want drop lines for the same reason. The same violet marks the current frame's entry in the breadcrumb, so the cue and the path agree. (Specced: REF-01 to REF-10, MRK-01, MRK-12, CAM-25.)
  - **Label halo (decided 2026-10-16).** A 2 px outline in the ground colour round the glyphs keeps a label readable where it crosses an orbit ring or the frame cue, without the weight of a box. Mock-up 19 compared 0 to 3 px and a backing box. (Specced: LBL-05.)
  - **Belt (decided 2026-10-16).** A soft band whose density falls towards its edges, with a seeded scatter of faint points, reads as an asteroid belt where a hard-edged band did not. (Specced: MRK-18.)
  - **Exposure rings say what they are (decided 2026-10-16).** A period line under the ring's label and a chevron ahead of its identity mark show how fast it goes and which way. (Specced: MOT-02, MOT-14.)
  - **Marker spacing (decided 2026-10-16).** Labels are placed from the selection box, so they never move on hover or select. The condition bar and the group count start just outside the box. Bracket arms stop growing at a cap when a disc is close in. A shared orbit's pill stands for all its members, and the mark slides rather than jumps when a satellite folds. (Specced: LBL-01, SYM-09, SYM-12, SYM-13, HOV-06, INS-06.)
  - **Labels never overprint (decided 2026-10-13, from F-115).** They try four sides, drop their second line, then hide, in a priority order led by the selection and the hovered object. (Specced: LBL-01 to LBL-04.)
  - Ship and station symbology (decided, specced as SYM): NATO-style frames for affiliation, icons for ship type, and amplifiers for state, groups and identification. See *Symbology* (`symbology.md`).
  - Navigation is by stepped zoom with stops, focus jumps and bookmarks (specced: ZOOM). Important off-screen objects get edge indicators (candidate; specced for gates as EDGE-01).
  - How gate distance is shown on screen is left open until gate placement is tuned (section 6).
- **Inspection view (decided):** highlighting any object expands it into a semi-photoreal view. This applies to everything: bodies, stations, gates, sites and ships. Ships appear as blips because of the distances involved, not because they are kept abstract.
  - **Presentation (decided 2026-10-12): one inset, docked at the right of the map.** The inset no longer follows its marker: text that moves across the screen, even slowly, is awkward to read. The white corner brackets on the marker, and its orbit ring in white, show what is selected, so there is no leader line. There is no pinning: comparing objects side by side belongs in tables in the administration view, and a fixed dock with several insets would need its own layout rules. (Specced: INS-01 to INS-03, INS-07.)
  - **The inset is a sidebar (decided 2026-10-13, from playtest finding F-107).** Drawn over the map, it hid part of the view and left the frame body off-centre in what was visible. Now it shrinks the map area, and the view slides with it so the frame body stays centred. Its full height leaves room for more about the selection later.
  - **"In orbit" is an outline of the whole system (decided 2026-10-13, from F5-07).** A flat list of the selection's satellites vanished as soon as one was clicked, so a single click had to wait out the double-click window. A tree from the star down stays put when you click, so a click selects at once, and moving to a parent or a sibling is one click. (Specced: INS-05, INS-07.)
  - **Fidelity follows knowledge (candidate):** the view shows the best image the faction holds. An unsurveyed body is a grainy telescope frame. A surveyed one is a clean render with overlays (ice, ruins, settlements). An old observation shows the last image with its date. Own ships and stations render fully; foreign contacts only as well as they have been observed. This ties the visuals to the uncertainty pillar and to partly known salvage sites (*Salvage & Research*). (Only placeholder surveyed and unsurveyed images are specced, INS-04: how information is gathered isn't designed yet, so there is no stale state, status text or date. Foreign contacts not yet.)
  - **Renders from designs (candidate):** ship and station renders are assembled from their component designs. So two bids for the same requirements look visibly different, and as-built variants and refits show.
  - **Planet surfaces are procedural (direction, 2026-10-17).** A shader draws each planet from the figures the generator already gives it (kind, gravity, equilibrium temperature, atmosphere class, water) and a seed derived from the sector seed and the body's id, so a body looks the same each time it is inspected. No art assets. Mock-up 29 shows seven kinds, four states of knowledge, phase and star colour, and close zoom.
    - **Knowledge is one value.** Fewer noise octaves and no small craters give a body seen from far away, and blur and grain on top give the telescope frame. The same large forms stay put as the faction learns more, so the unsurveyed and surveyed images are plainly the same planet. The planetary view's globe uses the same shader and adds octaves as the view closes.
    - **Light (decided 2026-10-17, from review of mock-up 29):** a sharp day-night terminator and almost no ambient light, for a photographic look. Atmospheres glow at the limb on the lit side only.
    - **Why:** the generator already produces the figures, art for every kind of body would be a large cost, and a procedural surface can show knowledge as a continuous value instead of a set of pictures.
    - **Still open:** the look needs tuning per kind (the ice giant is the plainest, and the molten kind's cracked crust is a guess), and nothing is specced.
- **Information model (decided):** the player has a semi-omniscient view. Signal delay isn't modelled: what the faction knows, the player sees at once, in every system.
- **Abstract uncertainty (candidate):** limits on knowledge are modelled abstractly rather than through physics.
  - Undetected contacts aren't shown. Thermal detection (see *Component Catalogue*, sensors) decides what appears.
  - Tracked contacts carry an uncertainty region showing tracking quality: sensor coverage, the contact's emission control, time since a good detection.
  - Salvage contents, foreign designs and unsurveyed bodies are known only as well as they have been observed (see the inspection view above).
- **Clutter (candidate):**
  - Moons fold into a neutral ring behind a body's marker, with a dim "+N" on its label; ships, groups and stations fold into a small pip down and to the right, which pops out into a count marker near the pointer or when the body is selected (specced: GRP; decided 2026-10-16, from F-244, F-229 and F-239, reasoning in *Symbology* 4).
  - Asteroid belts and debris fields appear as density shading. Surveyed, claimed or active sites get individual markers.
  - Information layers can be switched on and off: ownership, resources, salvage, traffic, sensor coverage.
- **Sky (direction, 2026-10-17):** a star field and Milky Way behind the system and sector views, to give a sense of scale: the sector is a small part of a galaxy. Procedural, with no art assets. Mock-up 28 compares styles. **Specced** in `docs/spec/sky.md` as SKY-01 to SKY-24, with the tunables.
  - **One sky for the whole sector, from a seed, tied to no system.** The galaxy is far off, so every system sees the same sky; Sol is no exception. The seed is derived from the sector seed, so each game has its own sky (SKY-02). It is not drawn from the sector's own stars. The flattened sector is a playability concession (*World Generator* 1), and true bearings to its neighbours would show it as a band of unusually bright stars. Neighbours stay on the sector view and the gate edge tabs.
  - **Drawn at infinity.** It turns with heading and tilt and never moves on pan or zoom, so it reads as far away. Beside the north dial it is the other cue for rotation, and it fits the non-rotating reference frame.
  - **Stars:** soft points, placed between pixels so they don't flicker as the view turns. Magnitude comes from a steep distribution (about 2.5 times as many stars for each magnitude fainter), so there are a few bright stars, some medium and a faint majority. Brighter stars are larger. Light is compressed so the faintest star sits about 20 levels above the ground and the brightest stays near half the neutral marker colour, so no star competes with a marker or a selection bracket.
  - **Milky Way:** a denser population of faint stars along a band, tilted 45° to 75° off the orbital plane by the seed and always crossing the opening view (SKY-07, SKY-08), so it crosses the screen at a slant and swings round as the player rotates. It is brighter towards one core and has a few dark lanes.
  - **Style (decided: B).** Stars with a very faint glow along the band (about 15 levels over the ground), so the band reads at a glance. Stars only (A) shows the band by density alone. Photographic (C), with strong colour and a bright core, is the loudest thing on screen and weakens the orbit rings and faint markers, so it is rejected for the map.
  - **Star colour (decided): a natural spread, kept pale.** Blue-white to orange, pulled most of the way back to neutral, so no star reads as an affiliation colour or the warning orange (*Interface Style* 2). The glow is neutral grey-blue.
  - **No text cue (decided).** The sky carries no labels.
  - **Against the belt grain:** the belt (MRK-18) is also faint points. Most of the sky is dimmer than the grain, though the brightest few stars are a little brighter (`sky_peak`). The grain sits in a ring round the star and moves with the map, and the sky holds still when the view pans, which tells them apart.
  - **Always on, for now (decided 2026-10-17).** It has no toggle until layer toggles are designed. It sits below everything in the draw order and is never a hover or click target.
  - **Both views (decided 2026-10-17).** The sector view draws the same sky at half strength (`sector_sky_factor`), so it stays quieter than the sector's own stars, which are outlined circles where the sky's are filled points (SKY-21, SKY-23, SKY-24).
- **Motion (candidate):** ship paths are drawn as their actual arcs, including burns: solid behind the ship, dashed ahead, and dashed from the departure point for a scheduled transfer. Transfer planning shows one live preview path with the arrival point on the target's ring, plus slider tracks coloured by delta-v; this replaces the earlier fan of labelled alternative paths (*Orbital Transfers* 3.3). How fast-orbiting objects are drawn is set by the motion rule (specced: MOT).
- **Sector view (decided):** the same style as the system view, with systems as nodes and gates as links. Gate throughput, reliability and ownership could show as line weight, line style and colour (candidate). **Specced** 2026-10-16 as a minimal truth view that shows every star and every link by state, whatever anyone knows: working solid, dormant dashed, collapsed faint (`docs/spec/sector-view.md`). A view filtered by knowledge replaces it later. Why: the generator's output has to be seen and checked before anything is hidden from the player.

### Navigation, satellites and motion

> **Specced** in `docs/spec/system-view.md`: navigation and zoom as ZOOM-01 to ZOOM-11, satellite grouping as GRP-01 to GRP-08, the motion rule as MOT-01 to MOT-12, with the tunables table. This section keeps the reasoning and the parts not yet specced.

- **Why:** the first prototype drew every moon at whole-system zoom, moons orbited too fast to read at 1 day/s, and fully continuous zoom felt odd. Hence:
  - logarithmic stepped zoom, with stops computed from each system's real orbits;
  - satellites fold into a "+N" count marker on their parent below a screen-radius threshold;
  - a motion rule based on apparent period (orbital period ÷ time rate) that shows fast orbits as bright "exposure rings" rather than smeared dots. Pausing or slowing time brings the dots back.
  - **Tails on the ring (decided 2026-10-12).** The first build drew dot-mode trails as a chain of sampled points, which looked bad. Instead the orbit ring itself carries the tail as a brightness gradient behind the dot, so it is one smooth stroke and reinforces the ring as the click target. The tail covers the arc swept in the last few real seconds, so it lengthens as time speeds up; when it reaches a full circle it simply is the exposure ring. One continuous rule replaces the two thresholds and the cross-fade between them. 
    Tails stay visible when paused (decided 2026-10-12), since that's when the map is studied: tail length uses the selected rate, which pausing keeps, and while paused each tail is capped just short of a full circle so ring-mode bodies drop back to dots. The bright head end still shows direction. (Specced: MOT-01, MOT-02, MOT-04, MOT-10, MOT-11, MOT-12, `tail_secs`, `dot_fade_from_degrees`.) A change of rate eases the tail to its new length over a short time rather than snapping (decided 2026-10-13, from playtest finding F-105; Specced: MOT-13, `tail_ease_ms`).
  - **Exposure rings vs. selection (decided 2026-10-13).** A full-brightness exposure ring looked like the white selected ring. For now exposure rings are partly transparent, so only the selected ring is full white (Specced: MOT-02, `exposure_ring_alpha`). Candidate for later, if that isn't enough: give the selected ring a soft wide halo instead, which keeps exposure rings bright.
- **Not yet specced (direction):**
  - Changing layer, to the sector view or a planetary view, is always deliberate: the breadcrumb, a double-click on the focused body, or the inset button. Scrolling never changes layer.
  - The motion rule also applies to the orbital layer of the planetary view.
  - A trajectory whose target is in exposure-ring mode ends on the ring; with a dot, it ends at the predicted arrival position.
  - The sector view uses the same navigation rules where they fit.

### Camera

> **Specced** in `docs/spec/system-view.md` as CAM-01 to CAM-25, with ZOOM-13 to ZOOM-17 (anchor, limits, stops, double-click) and CLK-05 (time-rate keys). Optimised for keyboard and mouse; gamepad and touch are kept possible but not specified.

- **Camera model (decided): a map camera with limited tilt.** The camera always looks at a pivot on the orbital plane. Rotation about the plane's normal is free. Tilt runs from straight down to a limit well short of the horizon, and the camera never goes below the plane. The **standard orientation** is north up with a slight tilt, not flat top-down.
  - *Why:* orbits are coplanar and markers are fixed-size, so a free 3D camera adds little except ways to get lost. Some tilt gives depth and matches the planetary-view transition, which is why a pure top-down view was rejected.
- **Rotation (decided): free, with a one-key reset** to the standard orientation; Home resets it too. Neither key is needed in normal play: the mouse alone should always get you back to a clean view (see zoom-out below). A fixed north would help spatial memory, but free rotation lets the player look across the system from any side, and the reset gives the memory aid back on demand.
- **Selection and reference frame (decided): two separate states, and the frame only changes deliberately.**
  - **Selection** is the object whose details are shown (inset, white brackets). A click sets it and Esc clears it. It never moves the camera.
  - **Reference frame (decided 2026-10-13)** is the object whose non-rotating frame the camera sits in; the camera tracks it as it moves. There is always one, and by default it is the star. This replaces "focus" with an optional object: "nothing focused" was always the star's frame, so making that explicit removes the special case. The player-facing verb stays "Focus" (F and the inset button); "frame" is the word in the docs and code.
    - *Why it exists:* at true scale with the clock running, a planet crosses the screen in seconds once you're zoomed in. At the system fit, drift is slow enough to read. So the frame matters exactly when you're close.
    - Changed only by double-click, F (on the selection), the inset's Focus button, the breadcrumb, or a bookmark. **Zooming never changes it**, except that reaching the system fit returns it to the star. Panning moves an offset from the frame body, clamped so the body stays on screen.
    - **Shown in the breadcrumb** as a path from the system down to the frame body ("Sector / Corvane / Halden IV / Arden Highport"). Clicking an ancestor moves into its frame, so you can climb several levels in one click. This replaces the focus chip. The sector sits at the top of the same path: changing scale from sector to system to body should feel like one interface, not separate screens, so crumbs need no per-layer icons.
    - **Selection and hover are corner brackets, and the frame body has no mark on the map (decided 2026-10-16, from F-160 and F-161).** A solid white ring round a marker read as one more circle: round a planet it looked like a satellite marker, and round an own ship's frame like a second frame. Four white corner brackets say "selected" without that clash, and hover is the same brackets dimmer. The frame body used to carry neutral brackets; that would now be confusable with selection, and the breadcrumb already names the frame. The box clears what is drawn, so it grows with a body's true-size disc. (Specced: HOV-06, HOV-07; CAM-22 is withdrawn.)
    - **Planetary view (direction):** it opens from inside a body's frame, so it can appear as one more crumb ("… / Halden IV / Surface"). Not specced until the planetary view is better defined.
    - **Ships in transit (direction):** they have no parent body, so their frame hangs off the star's (their breadcrumb is "Corvane / *ship*"), not a special no-frame case. Not specced until transit is.
    - **Breadcrumb drop-downs (candidate):** each crumb could list its children, for sideways moves between sibling moons. Overlaps the inset's "In orbit" list.
    - **Zoom stops are called "fits"** (system fit, satellite fit), so "frame" means only the reference frame.
  - **Select by orbit ring (decided 2026-10-12).** Every drawn orbit is a click target for its object, and highlights on hover and selection. The ring stands still while the body moves, so selection no longer means chasing a dot. That lets bodies stay dots at higher speeds, so the ring-mode threshold came down. Near the star and round a busy parent, rings crowd: the nearest ring wins, and the hover highlight is what tells the player which one they'll get. (Specced: HOV-02, HOV-03; the threshold is now `tail_secs`.)
  - **Why selection and frame stay separate with a single inset (decided 2026-10-12).** Merging them would make every click move the camera. And a body in ring mode can be selected by its ring but can't be a frame, so the two states must differ.
  - **Fast bodies can't be a frame.** A body in exposure-ring mode has no readable position, so double-click or F selects it and moves the frame to its parent. If the frame body enters ring mode (the clock speeds up), the frame moves to its parent, and never comes back on its own: a change of clock rate shouldn't move the camera twice.
  - **Zoom has two stops, both meaningful:** the system fit (everything) and the frame body's satellite fit (its neighbourhood). Every other notch is a plain step. Zoom anchors on the frame body, or on the cursor in the star's frame.
  - **Inner limit:** in a body's frame, just before its true disc would fill a large part of the screen. Close enough, the body is drawn at its true size instead of as a marker. In the star's frame, a fixed minimum view radius: you can zoom in on a point and watch bodies drift past, and double-click is the way to get closer to anything.
  - **Zooming out to the system fit resets the view:** the pivot eases back to the star and the tilt to standard, as part of the same zoom, keeping the heading. The wheel alone always brings the whole system back.
  - Double-click on the frame body opens the planetary view: first "look at it", then "go there".
  - Keeping selection and frame apart is useful: you can stay on Halden IV while reading about IVc or K-3, without the camera jumping to a fast moon.
  - *Why two states:* an earlier draft had three (selection; focus as a one-off framing move; follow as persistent tracking). Focus and follow were always coupled, "double-click an already-inspected body" was ambiguous, and selection still moved the camera through the zoom anchor, so a selected but untracked planet could be zoomed into and immediately fly off screen.
  - *Rejected:* selection also focuses, as in Stellaris. Clicking a fast moon would swing the camera onto it, and you could no longer read about one thing while watching another.
  - *Rejected:* zooming in sets focus. It changed what the camera followed as a side effect of scrolling, and which body you got depended on the cursor. Playing it felt unpredictable.
  - *Rejected:* a "region frame" stop between the system and satellite fits (the inner system, or a frame round the focused planet's orbit). It was vague and arbitrary; plain zoom steps do its job.
- **Magnifier (rejected):** a dashed circle that fanned out a selected planet's folded satellites in true geometry. It looked bad on screen, and once double-click focused and framed a planet in one move, it only showed the same moons at a second scale. The inset's "In orbit" list (INS-05) replaces it: it shows what is in orbit without moving the camera. A selected folded satellite rings its parent's pill (INS-06).
- **Mouse (decided):** left drag and middle drag pan 1:1 under the cursor, with no inertia. Right drag rotates (horizontal = heading, vertical = tilt). **Right-click is reserved** for context menus and orders; a small movement threshold tells it apart from right drag. Shift+left drag is kept free for a possible box select.
- **Keyboard (decided):** WASD and the arrows pan at a speed relative to the screen, so it feels the same at every zoom. Q/E rotate, Page Up/Down tilt, + and − zoom one step. **The time rate uses `.` (faster) and `,` (slower)**, so + and − can zoom, as is common in strategy games. Edge scrolling exists but is off by default. All bindings live in one table so they can be rebound later.
- **Bounds (decided):** the pivot can't leave the system's neighbourhood, so the player can't get lost in empty space. Home always recovers.
- **Per-system camera memory (direction):** leaving a system for the sector or a planetary view and coming back restores the camera, including the frame. Not specced: the sector view remembers its own camera (SECT-32), but a system always opens at its system fit (SECT-31).
- **Rotating frame (candidate):** the camera could optionally rotate with the frame body's orbit, so the star stays in one direction. Useful for planning transfers.
- **Selection cycling (candidate):** Tab and Shift+Tab step the selection through bodies in orbit order. Useful on the keyboard, and the main way to navigate with a gamepad.
- **Sector view camera (direction):** the sector map is 3D, but shallow. The same camera model applies, with the pivot on the sector's mean plane. Tilt there carries real information, so it may want a wider range. Because depth is shallow, drop lines from each system to the mean plane (or a faint plane grid) could make height readable at a glance (candidate). **Specced** 2026-10-16: the same camera with the pivot on the mean plane and the standard tilt, no drop lines yet (SECT-10 to SECT-12).
- **Gamepad and touch (candidate):** every camera action should be reachable without a mouse.
  - Gamepad: left stick pans, right stick rotates and tilts, triggers step zoom, shoulders cycle selection, and a virtual cursor picks.
  - Touch: one-finger drag pans, pinch zooms, two-finger twist rotates, two-finger vertical drag tilts, double-tap stands in for double-click.
  - Nothing should be reachable only by hover.

## 3. Planetary view

- **Purpose (decided):** where settlements are administered and ground combat takes place.
- **Entry (direction):** from the system view, by the inset's "Open planetary view" button or by double-clicking the focused body; never by scrolling. The inspection view is a look at a body; the planetary view is where the player acts on it.
- **Style (candidate):** a globe that uses the body's photoreal render as its surface, with vector overlays for settlements, infrastructure, extraction sites, ground formations, landing sites and orbital assets overhead. This joins the two visual styles in one view.
- **Ground combat (candidate):** delegated and lightly simulated, like space combat (*Combat, Raiders & Diplomacy*). The player sets objectives and posture, and watches formations move on the overlay.
- **Coverage (candidate):** a full planetary view only for bodies with settlements, surface sites or fighting. Small moons and asteroids use the inspection view alone.

## 4. Faction administration view

- **Purpose (decided):** managing the faction at a high level: policy, budget, appointments, organisations, collated reports and character interactions.
- **Style (direction):** a fully 2D UI, separate from the spatial layers. Layout, panels, message traffic, reports, requests and dossiers: *Interface Style* (`ui-style.md`, candidate).
- **Links to the map (candidate):** reports, requests and alerts link to the place they concern, and spatial objects link back to their owners, staff and records.
- **Characters (candidate):** shown with what the faction knows about them, keeping "average" and "unknown" visibly distinct (*Characters, Organisations & Events*).

## 5. Art scope (direction)

The vector layer is cheap to produce, and so is the sky: it is generated, with no art. Planet and moon surfaces are generated too (section 2, planet surfaces), so photoreal art is needed only for stations, gates, ruins and ship components. The administration view needs portraits and 2D interface art. This keeps art cost in line with the scope risk (design document, section 7).
