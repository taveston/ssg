# Orphan Worlds: Characters, Organisations & Events

**In short:** the player rules through people and institutions. Characters are people within the state, not a dynasty: skills say how well they do things, opposing trait pairs say what they choose to do, and much of it is hidden until revealed by behaviour. The player appoints only senior posts. Organisations act independently and drive the private economy; the player can take direct control of anything, at a price. There are no canned pop-up events: requests and problems arise from the simulation.

*Status:* draft. Status tags as in the design document (**Decided**, **Direction**, **Candidate**). Companion documents: *Design Document* (overview, pillar 3), *Economy* (what organisations own), *Factions & Ideology* (the triangle characters share), *Requirements & Bids* (5.3, trait rules in bids), *Interface Style* (dossiers).

## 1. Characters (direction)

People within the state (governors, commanders, company heads, scientists), not a dynasty. To avoid copying Crusader Kings 3, traits are behavioural rules rather than stat bundles, and some information is hidden. Key figures get individuality from goals, history and relationships; minor characters stay light.

- **Skills (candidate):** how well a character does things. Administration, Industry, Science, Command, Diplomacy, each 1 to 10.
- **Traits (decided for now):** what a character chooses to do. Opposing pairs: Ambitious/Content, Bold/Cautious, Principled/Opportunistic, Innovative/Traditionalist, possibly Ruthless/Humane.
  - At most one of each pair (none means average); usually two or three in all; mostly fixed.
  - More pairs can be added if a system needs them: Idealist/Pragmatist, Secretive/Forthcoming, Frugal/Extravagant, Territorial/Cooperative.
- **Ideology (candidate):** characters hold a position in the same ideology triangle as factions, with the same pool of 12 and the same labels ("a paternalist governor"). Differences between a character, the population and government policy create friction. See *Factions & Ideology*.
- **Relationships (candidate):** opinion, grievances, debts. Separate from personality: the memory of how the player treated someone.
- **Uncertainty (candidate):** skills are known roughly and traits are revealed through behaviour. The UI must tell "average" apart from "unknown".

## 2. Roles (direction)

- The player appoints only senior posts: cabinet; leaders of colonies, settlements, bases and stations; admirals; captains; ground formation leaders.
- The player doesn't appoint organisation staff unless the organisation is state-owned.
- *Candidates:* elected posts depending on settlement type and civic alignment; a legislature represented by a few bloc leaders.

## 3. Organisations (direction)

- Yards, mining and shipping firms, institutes, often remnants from before the collapse.
- They are subjects of the faction but act independently and drive the private economy (*Economy*). Very powerful ones might break away (later).
- The player works through policy, contracts, favour and pressure.
- In the setting, firms are corporations, guilds and institutes; "organisation" is the mechanical term.

## 4. Direct control (candidate)

The player can always take direct control at a price: an efficiency penalty, expenditure or a cost in relations. Requisition and state ownership are the far end of this.

## 5. Events (direction)

- No canned pop-up events. Instead:
  - requests and problems that arise from the simulated state and agendas;
  - reports from real data;
  - choices that differ in kind, with uncertain or delayed consequences;
  - lore as optional recovered records.
- Only the Earth mystery and the external threat get a few authored beats (decided). See *Setting & Back Story*.
