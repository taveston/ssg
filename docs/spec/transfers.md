# Spec: Transfer simulation

**In short:** ships fly on rails: a trajectory is a list of segments (parked, conic, spiral), each evaluated analytically from the clock. A transfer between two places is one crossing round a primary, with the planet-centred legs collapsed to a delta-v cost and a few days at each end. Impulsive drives get a two-burn Lambert arc; low-thrust drives get an Edelbaum spiral of fixed length. Bodies never bend a coasting ship. A destination is a place (a band at a body, a station, a gate, a site, a ship), never a bare orbit. Ships in a band sit at real places round it. Inside a body's frame a crossing flies between real places, and a ship's own band is met by a phasing leg: whole laps of a slightly different orbit, trading delta-v for time. At the edge of a frame phase only sets a wait: for the exit point on leaving, for the target on arriving. A group is one place. Orders are the save-game truth and trajectories are recomputed from them.

*Scope:* the simulation of transfers: the trajectory model, the solver, places and bands, phase round a band, orders and their data. No UI: the planner and what it draws are in `docs/spec/planner.md`, and alerts, the return reserve, multi-leg plans, groups, gates' entry rule, re-tasking in flight, gravity assists and tactical rules are not specced (see *Not yet specced*). *Source ideas:* `docs/ideas/mechanics/transfers.md` sections 2, 4 (phase is 4.4) and 8, with figures from `docs/ideas/mechanics/drives-and-transit.md` sections 2 and 3. **Built** is ✓ once a requirement is in the code.

*Retired IDs* (never reuse): none.

## 1. Rails (RAIL)

A ship's position and velocity at any day come from its trajectory, never from integration.

| ID | Requirement | Built |
|---|---|---|
| RAIL-01 | A `Trajectory` is an ordered list of segments. Each `Segment` has `start_day`, `end_day` (which may be unbounded) and a `kind`. Segments are contiguous: each starts on the day the previous one ends, and the last segment is unbounded. | ✓ |
| RAIL-02 | Segment kinds: **Parked** `{ orbit }` (a reference to a named orbit, SCL-07: position and velocity are the orbit's at the ship's phase); **Conic** `{ primary, elements }` (an ellipse or hyperbola round the primary); **Spiral** `{ primary, r0, r1, θ0, θ1, v0, accel }` (a low-thrust crossing, RAIL-07). | ✓ |
| RAIL-03 | Position and velocity are stored and returned in km and km/day, `f64`, relative to the segment's primary (SCL-01, ARCH-10). Delta-v budgets and costs are in km/s; one named function converts between km/day and km/s. | ✓ |
| RAIL-04 | A ship's state on day *d* is read from the one segment whose span contains *d* (a segment owns its start day, not its end day). Evaluating a trajectory for any day in its span is a pure function of the trajectory and the day (ARCH-20). | ✓ |
| RAIL-05 | **Conic** elements are the orbital plane's (XZ, the plane every body orbits in, SCL-04): semi-latus rectum *p* (km), eccentricity *e*, argument of periapsis ω (rad, measured in the sense of motion), time of periapsis *t_p* (days), and the sense of motion (prograde or retrograde). Position and velocity on any day come from Kepler's equation in its elliptic (e < 1) or hyperbolic (e > 1) form, or a universal-variable form that covers both. A conic built from a state has *e* at least `parabola_epsilon` away from 1 (the state's *e* is nudged to the nearer side), so there is no parabolic branch. | ✓ |
| RAIL-06 | A conic is built from a state (position, velocity, day) and the primary's gravitational parameter, and a state read back from it on that day reproduces the state to within 1 km and 1 km/day. Holds for ellipses and hyperbolae in both senses of motion. | ✓ |
| RAIL-07 | **Spiral** evaluation: with *s* = days since `start_day`, the speed is *v(s)* = *v0* ∓ `accel`·*s* (minus when climbing to a higher orbit, plus when descending) and the radius is *r(s)* = μ / *v(s)*²; the angle is θ(s) = θ0 + ∫₀ˢ √(μ/r³) ds + *k*·*s*, where *k* is the one constant that makes θ(end) = θ1 (so the spiral winds at the local circular rate and lands where the target is). The velocity is the curve's time derivative. The integral is evaluated in closed form or by a fixed quadrature: no per-frame integration. | ✓ |
| RAIL-08 | A coasting ship keeps its conic: no body other than the segment's primary changes a Conic or Spiral segment, however close the path comes to it. Passing through a body's Hill sphere changes nothing in the simulation. | ✓ |
| RAIL-09 | A ship on a Conic or Spiral segment is **in flight**; a ship on a Parked segment is a member of its named orbit (SCL-07) and nothing else. | ✓ |

## 2. Places and bands (BAND)

A destination is a place. A place resolves to one named orbit (SCL-07) and a body that charges for leaving and arriving.

| ID | Requirement | Built |
|---|---|---|
| BAND-01 | Every star, planet and moon has two bands, which are named orbits (SCL-07): **Low** at radius = mean radius (SCL-05) + `low_band_altitude_km`, and **High** at radius = min(`high_band_radii` × mean radius, `high_band_hill_fraction` × Hill radius), where the Hill radius is *a* (*m* / 3*M*)^⅓ for the body's orbit radius *a*, mass *m* and parent mass *M*. The star has no Hill radius, so its High band is `high_band_radii` × its radius. A body whose High radius would not exceed its Low radius has no High band. | |
| BAND-02 | A place is one of: **Body** `{ body, band }`, the band being Low or High (a band of a star, planet or moon); **Station** `{ station }` (the band the station occupies, at the station's phase, SCL-07); **Gate** `{ gate }` (the gate's own orbit); **Site** `{ site }` (a belt or salvage site's own orbit); **Ship** `{ ship }` (wherever the ship is on the arrival day); **Hold** (no destination: stop burning, PLAN-13). | ✓ |
| BAND-03 | The **departure cost** of a place in a named orbit of radius *r* round a body of gravitational parameter μ is *v_circ*(√2 − 1) = √(μ/r)(√2 − 1), in km/s: the cost of escaping a circular orbit to rest at infinity. The **capture cost** of arriving at a place is the same figure. A place whose orbit has no massive body (a gate, a site, a ship in flight) costs 0. | ✓ |
| BAND-04 | The **approach days** of a place are (Hill radius − *r*) ÷ √(2μ/*r*), converted to days: the time to coast from the orbit to the body's Hill radius at the orbit's escape speed. Zero for a place with no massive body, for a body with no Hill sphere (the star), and where the orbit lies outside the Hill sphere (the difference would be negative). Both the departure and the arrival end of a crossing add their place's approach days (XFER-02). | ✓ |
| BAND-05 | Ships in one named orbit are not necessarily co-located (the ships of a group are, PHAS-14). Each is at its orbit's position for its own phase (RAIL-02), so two ships whose phases differ are 2*r* sin(gap ÷ 2) apart on every day (PHAS-01), and every rule that reads where a ship is reads that place. Which orbit a ship is a member of, and what it costs to leave or enter it, do not depend on phase; the time can (PHAS-03, PHAS-10). | |
| BAND-06 | A ship that captures (a Rendezvous) into a named orbit takes the phase of its **capture point**: the closest point of the prograde hyperbola it arrives on, whose closest distance is the orbit's radius *r* and whose speed far from the body is *v∞*, the magnitude of (arc end velocity − body velocity). With α the angle of that velocity in the orbital plane and *e* = 1 + *r* *v∞*² ÷ μ, the capture point is at the angle α − arccos(1 ÷ *e*) on the arrival day. A Rendezvous at a station or a ship is timed so the ship arrives at that point together with its target (PHAS-10). A ship placed by content or a generator takes the phase it is given. | |
| BAND-07 | A ship that is not in a named orbit is **coasting**: its state is its rail's, and its place is described by what it last did (the plan that ended there, PLAN-12). Nothing can be ordered from a coasting state except a new plan starting from it. | ✓ |
| BAND-08 | A coasting ship on a conic round the star with *e* ≥ 1, or a bound conic whose apoapsis exceeds `lost_beyond_au`, is **escaping**. An escaping ship with no plan is removed from the simulation when its distance from the star first exceeds `lost_beyond_au`; what the game says about it first is for the alerts spec. | ✓ |

## 3. Transfers (XFER)

A transfer is a crossing from a start state to a target place, solved once when ordered and never adjusted.

| ID | Requirement | Built |
|---|---|---|
| XFER-01 | The **primary** of a crossing is the lowest common ancestor of the departure place's body and the destination's body in the system tree (SCL-03): the star for planet-to-planet, the planet for band-to-band, moon-to-moon and station-to-moon trips round it. The crossing's start state is that of the departure place's highest ancestor below the primary on the crossing's start day (a ship leaving a moon for another planet starts from its planet's state), and its end state is the destination's highest ancestor below the primary on the crossing's end day. A place whose body is the crossing's primary (a band of it, or a station, gate or site in an orbit round it) charges no departure or capture cost and has no approach days, so a trip between two bands of one body has the body as primary and costs only its burns. A gate or a site is aimed at by its own orbit's state, not by that of the body it orbits. When the destination is a band of the primary itself, its end state is on the band's circle, half a turn ahead of the start state's angle, so a Hohmann travel time gives the Hohmann burns. | ✓ |
| XFER-02 | A leg's timing, in days: **crossing start** = `depart_day` + departure wait (PHAS-03) + departure approach days (BAND-04) + burn days (XFER-05); **crossing end** = crossing start + `travel_days`; **arrival** = crossing end + arrival approach days + arrival wait (PHAS-10). The ship's rail is Parked in its departure orbit until the crossing start, then the crossing segment, then (for a Rendezvous) Parked in the destination orbit from the crossing end, with its phase set so that on the arrival day it is at the capture point (BAND-06). The plan is InFlight from `depart_day` to arrival (PLAN-10). A phasing leg (PHAS-05) has its own timing (PHAS-09). | |
| XFER-03 | **Impulsive crossing.** For a drive with `impulsive = true`, the crossing is the prograde, zero-revolution Lambert arc from the start state's position at the crossing start to the end state's position at the crossing end, with transfer angle in (0°, 360°). Burn 1 = the magnitude of (arc start velocity − start state velocity); burn 2 = the magnitude of (end state velocity − arc end velocity), in km/s. | ✓ |
| XFER-04 | **Leg delta-v.** Rendezvous: departure cost + burn 1 + burn 2 + capture cost. Intercept: departure cost + burn 1. A leg whose delta-v exceeds the ship's available delta-v (PLAN-21) is infeasible. | ✓ |
| XFER-05 | **Burn days** for an impulsive drive = (departure cost + burn 1) ÷ `accel`, in days, computed from a solve with zero burn days and no departure wait (PHAS-03); the crossing is then solved once more from the later start, and that second solve's burns are the leg's. A low-thrust drive has zero burn days (its costs are in the spiral, XFER-06). | |
| XFER-06 | **Low-thrust crossing.** For a drive with `impulsive = false`, the crossing is a Spiral (RAIL-07) between the start and end states' circular orbits round the primary. Its delta-v = departure cost + *Δv_c* + capture cost (Rendezvous) or departure cost + *Δv_c* (Intercept), where *Δv_c* is the magnitude of (*v_circ*(r0) − *v_circ*(r1)), and `travel_days` = that delta-v ÷ `accel`, in days: fixed by the drive, not chosen. The spiral's *v0* is the start orbit's circular speed and it ends at the end state's angle on the crossing end day. It spans the whole crossing, whose `travel_days` also pays the departure and capture costs, so its `accel` (RAIL-07) is *Δv_c* ÷ `travel_days`: lower than the drive's. A low-thrust crossing is only solved between circular start and end states; from a coasting state it is infeasible. | ✓ |
| XFER-07 | **No plan hits its primary.** A crossing is infeasible if its conic passes too close to its primary, and so is an Intercept whose coast does. Too close is within `min_periapsis_radii` × the primary's mean radius: the conic's periapsis is inside that radius and is reached during the crossing, or is still ahead on the coast. A crossing or coast that starts or ends in a place round the primary itself (a band, or a station in one) has the Low band's radius (BAND-01) as its limit instead: its periapsis may not be lower than that. Such a crossing can start or end at the Low band, but can't dip under it, so no plan flies through a body. Only the primary is tested, because no other body touches a path (RAIL-08). | |
| XFER-08 | **Arrival modes.** **Rendezvous** captures: burn 2 is made, the capture cost paid, and the ship joins the destination's named orbit (BAND-06). **Intercept** `{ band }` skips both: the crossing segment is extended without end and the ship coasts (BAND-07). An Intercept takes the High or the Low band, and an Intercept through Low turns the ship (XFER-13). An Intercept has no arrival approach days. | |
| XFER-09 | **Relative speed at an Intercept** = √(*v∞*² + 2μ/*r*), where *v∞* = the magnitude of (arc end velocity − body velocity) and *r* is the intercept band's radius; reported in km/s. | ✓ |
| XFER-10 | **Ship as destination.** A station is a target as a parked ship is, at its phase. A target in the ship's own named orbit is a phasing leg (PHAS-05). A target parked in another orbit of the crossing's primary has as its end state its own state on the crossing end day, so it is met where it is. A target parked in an orbit of any other body is aimed at as that body is (XFER-01): a Rendezvous is timed to meet it (PHAS-10); an Intercept is aimed at the body, takes the target's band and is trimmed to meet the target (PHAS-15). A coasting target's end state is its rail state on the crossing end day (its primary must be the crossing's primary, else infeasible). Rendezvous matches the target's velocity (burn 2) and the ship then shares the target's segment: its named orbit, at the target's phase, if the target is parked; a copy of its conic if the target is coasting. Intercept at a target whose state is the end state closes at the relative speed, the magnitude of (arc end velocity − target velocity). | |
| XFER-11 | The maths (conics, Lambert, the spiral, trajectory evaluation) is the `orbits` crate (ARCH-40). The game's rules are pure functions in `src/sim/transfer/` (`places.rs` for bands and costs, `legs.rs` for timing and delta-v, `plan.rs` for orders): states, places, a drive and a budget in, a leg with its trajectory and delta-v out, or an infeasibility reason. Nothing in it reads Bevy resources or knows about the view (ARCH-02, ARCH-20). *Checked in review.* | ✓ |
| XFER-12 | **Worked checks (must hold within 3%), in plain figures, as `orbits` crate tests:** a star of 0.75 solar masses; a planet of radius 5,753 km and surface gravity 0.82 g at 0.8 AU; an inner planet at 0.5 AU; a belt at 0.64 AU (the reference layout of `drives-and-transit.md` section 2, which is not the content's Corvane). The planet's Low band (500 km up) costs about 2.7 km/s to leave and its High band (10 radii) about 0.9 km/s; the Hohmann crossing 0.5 → 0.8 AU has burn 1 + burn 2 of about 7.5 km/s and takes about 111 days; 0.8 → 0.64 AU about 3.4 km/s and 129 days. | ✓ |
| XFER-13 | **Turn at an Intercept through Low.** An Intercept through a Low band (BAND-01) at a body other than the crossing's primary turns the ship's velocity relative to the body. Let *r* be the Low band's radius, **v∞** the relative velocity (arc end velocity − body velocity) and *e* = 1 + *r* *v∞*² ÷ μ. The turn is δ = 2 arcsin(1 ÷ *e*), in the prograde sense, as the hyperbola of BAND-06 goes round the body, and the size of **v∞** is unchanged. The crossing segment ends on the crossing end day. From that day the rail is a new Conic round the crossing's primary, from the body's position and with velocity = the body's velocity + **v∞** turned by δ. The turn costs no delta-v and no time. It is a rule of the Intercept, not a body changing a segment (RAIL-08). An Intercept through High takes no turn (its turn is small, 5° to 65° at 5 to 10 km/s, and is rounded off), and nor does an Intercept in the frame of the crossing's primary, whose conic is already round the body. | |
| XFER-14 | **Closing speed at a target in the band.** An Intercept at a station or a parked ship in an orbit of a body other than the crossing's primary (XFER-10) goes by the target in the sense of the band's motion (BAND-06), so at their closest point it passes the target at √(*v∞*² + 2μ/*r*) − √(μ/*r*) relative to it, in km/s, with *r* the band's radius. This is the closing speed. XFER-09's figure is the speed relative to the body. | |
| XFER-15 | **Worked checks (must hold within 3%), as `orbits` crate tests,** on the planet of XFER-12 (radius 5,753 km, 0.82 g), for an Intercept through its Low band (500 km up) arriving at 2, 5 and 10 km/s relative to it: the turn is 132°, 78° and 35°. At 5 km/s the speed relative to the body is 10.5 km/s and the closing speed at a station in the band is 4.0 km/s. The turn angle and the closing speed are functions of the `orbits` crate with plain-language docs and doctests (ARCH-42, ARCH-43). *Checked in review.* | |

## 4. Phase (PHAS)

A ship in an orbit has a place round it (BAND-05). Inside one body's frame a crossing flies between real places. At the edge of the frame the planet-centred leg is collapsed (XFER-01): it costs the same delta-v and the same delay whatever the phase, and phase only sets a wait, for the exit point on leaving and for the target on arriving. A ship's own orbit is met by a phasing leg: the ship moves to a slightly smaller or larger orbit, flies whole laps of it, and comes back to the ring where the target is.

| ID | Requirement | Built |
|---|---|---|
| PHAS-01 | The **gap** from one thing to another in the same named orbit is the angle, in [0, 2π), by which the other is ahead of it in the sense of motion. Everything in one orbit turns at the orbit's rate (RAIL-02), so a gap changes only when a ship burns. | |
| PHAS-02 | Two things in one named orbit are **together** when their separation, 2*r* sin(gap ÷ 2), is under `together_km`. A ship together with a station is docked at it. | |
| PHAS-03 | **Leaving waits for the exit point.** A leg of an impulsive drive that departs from an orbit of a body other than the crossing's primary starts from that body's state (XFER-01), but only once the ship is at its **exit point** in the orbit. Let *v∞* be the magnitude and α the angle of (arc start velocity − body velocity) in a solve with no wait and no burn days, *r* the orbit's radius and *e* = 1 + *r* *v∞*² ÷ μ: the exit point is at the angle α + arccos(1 ÷ *e*) − π. The wait is ((exit angle − φ) mod 2π) ÷ ω days, where φ is the ship's angle on `depart_day` and ω = 2π ÷ the orbit's period, so it is under one period. The crossing is then solved once more from its later start (XFER-05). A low-thrust drive has no wait. Costs and approach days do not depend on phase. | |
| PHAS-04 | **Already there.** A destination that is the ship's own named orbit (the Body place of its band, or a station or ship in it that is together with it) is already reached: the plan is Done at once, as a Hold is (PLAN-10), costs no delta-v and changes nothing. | |
| PHAS-05 | **Phasing leg.** A destination that is a station or a parked ship in the ship's own named orbit, and not together with it, is reached by a phasing leg: two burns at one point of the orbit, with whole laps of a coplanar ellipse that touches the orbit at that point between them. The body is the crossing's primary, so the leg has no departure or capture cost and no approach days (XFER-01). A phasing leg is always a Rendezvous: an Intercept is infeasible, and so is a low-thrust drive. | |
| PHAS-06 | **Meeting times.** Let *T* be the orbit's period, *r* its radius and *g* the gap from the ship to the target divided by 2π. The target next reaches the burn point *t_k* = *T*(*k* − *g*) days after the first burn, for *k* = 1, 2, 3, … In that time the ship flies *n* whole laps of its phasing orbit, whose period is *T_p* = *t_k* ÷ *n*: the **inner** orbit with *n* = *k* (period shorter than *T*; the burn point is its farthest point) or the **outer** orbit with *n* = *k* − 1 (period longer than *T*; the burn point is its closest point; needs *k* ≥ 2). Its semi-major axis is *a_p* = *r* (*T_p* ÷ *T*)^⅔. | |
| PHAS-07 | **Burns.** Each of the two burns is \|√(μ(2 ÷ *r* − 1 ÷ *a_p*)) − √(μ ÷ *r*)\| in km/s, and the leg's delta-v is their sum. Burn 1 is spent at the crossing start and burn 2 at the crossing end (PLAN-21). A leg whose delta-v exceeds the ship's available delta-v is infeasible (XFER-04). | |
| PHAS-08 | **Which orbit.** The leg's `travel_days` is a request. The solver takes the smallest *k* ≥ 1 with *t_k* ≥ `travel_days`, and of that *k*'s inner and outer orbits the feasible one with the smaller delta-v (the inner on a tie). An orbit is feasible when its closest point is no nearer the body than its Low band (BAND-01) and its farthest point no farther than the body's Hill radius (`lost_beyond_au` from the star). If neither is feasible it tries the next *k*, up to `max_phasing_laps`, and then the leg is infeasible. XFER-07 does not apply to a phasing leg. The leg's `travel_days` becomes *t_k*. Rebuilding from the order gives the same orbit (PLAN-03). | |
| PHAS-09 | **Timing and rail.** Crossing start = `depart_day` + burn days (XFER-05, from burn 1 alone); crossing end = crossing start + *t_k*; arrival = crossing end. The rail is Parked in its orbit until the crossing start, then a Conic round the body (the ellipse, built from the ship's state after burn 1, RAIL-06) to the crossing end, then Parked in the same orbit at the target's phase, so the ship is together with the target. The plan is InFlight from `depart_day` to arrival (PLAN-10). | |
| PHAS-10 | **Arrival wait.** A Rendezvous at a station or a parked ship whose orbit is not an orbit of the crossing's primary is timed so the ship arrives together with the target. Let *d₀* = crossing end + arrival approach days, φ the target's angle on *d₀*, ψ the capture point (BAND-06) and ω = 2π ÷ the orbit's period. The wait is ((ψ − φ) mod 2π) ÷ ω days, which is under one period of that orbit, and the ship's phase on the arrival day is ψ, the target's phase on that day. Any other arrival has no wait. | |
| PHAS-11 | **Intercept records its point.** An Intercept records its **closest-approach phase**: the angle of BAND-06's formula, with *r* the intercept band's radius. An Intercept at a body does not read the phase of a station or ship in the band it passes, and this spec does not decide whether it meets anything there. An Intercept at a station or a parked ship meets it (PHAS-15). | |
| PHAS-12 | The phasing orbit (period, semi-major axis and burn sizes from μ, *r*, *g*, *k* and the choice of inner or outer) and the capture-point angle (from μ, *r* and the arrival velocity) are functions of the `orbits` crate, with plain-language docs and doctests (ARCH-42, ARCH-43). Which *k* to take, feasibility against bands and the plan are in `src/sim/transfer/` (XFER-11). *Checked in review.* | |
| PHAS-13 | **Worked checks (must hold within 3%), in plain figures, as `orbits` crate tests,** on the planet of XFER-12 (radius 5,753 km, 0.82 g). The checks fix the High band at 10 radii, 57,530 km out; the `orbits` crate has no tunables, and the game's `high_band_radii` (BAND-01) may differ. That band has period 1.95 d, circular speed 2.15 km/s. Its Low band is 6,253 km out: period 1.67 h, 6.52 km/s. **High, target half a turn ahead:** *k* = 1 meets after 0.97 d, inner only, 1.54 km/s; *k* = 3 after 4.86 d, 0.29 km/s (inner, 3 laps of a 1.62 d orbit, or outer, 2 laps of a 2.43 d orbit); *k* = 10 after 18.5 d, 0.076 km/s. **Low, target half a turn ahead:** *k* = 1 is infeasible (an inner orbit dips below the band and *k* = 1 has no outer one); *k* = 2 meets after 2.5 h, outer, 1.46 km/s; *k* = 10 after 16 h, 0.23 km/s. **Rule of thumb, for *k* ≥ 2:** delta-v × *t_k* is within 3% of ⅔ × *r* × the angle the ship must make up, in radians (2π*g* for an inner orbit, 2π(1 − *g*) for an outer one). **Capture point at *v∞* = 5 km/s:** High band *e* = 6.40, at α − 81.0°; Low band *e* = 1.59, at α − 51.0°. **Exit point at *v∞* = 5 km/s:** High band at α − 99.0°; Low band at α − 129.0°. | |
| PHAS-14 | **Groups are together.** The ships of a group are together (PHAS-02) for as long as they are in it: one orbit, one phase. Ships that are not together can't be in one group: they close the gap first (PHAS-05). How a group is ordered is not specced (*Not yet specced*). | |
| PHAS-15 | **Intercept trim.** An Intercept at a station or a parked ship in an orbit of a body other than the crossing's primary (XFER-10) meets the target: it passes through the target's band, and the solver adds a **trim** to the leg's `travel_days` so that on the crossing end day the target is at the Intercept's closest-approach phase (PHAS-11). `travel_days` is the least the leg takes (as in PHAS-08), and the trim is under one period of the band. A different `travel_days` gives a different arrival velocity and so a different closest-approach phase, so the solver solves the leg again with each trimmed time, up to `trim_max_iterations` times, until the target is within `together_km` of the Intercept's point along the band. A leg that hasn't settled by then is infeasible. The leg's delta-v and timing are those of the trimmed leg. How a ship avoids hitting the target it meets is for combat. | |

## 5. Orders and status (PLAN)

Orders are the truth; a trajectory is derived from its order and can be rebuilt from it.

| ID | Requirement | Built |
|---|---|---|
| PLAN-01 | A `Plan { ship, legs, status }` holds one or more `Leg { destination, arrival, depart_day, travel_days, trajectory }`, where `arrival` is Rendezvous or Intercept `{ band }`. A plain transfer is a one-leg plan. Only one-leg plans are specced; a plan with more legs is rejected until multi-leg plans are. | ✓ |
| PLAN-02 | `status` is one of **Scheduled**, **InFlight**, **Holding**, **Done**, **Failed**. A ship has at most one plan that is Scheduled, InFlight or Holding. | ✓ |
| PLAN-03 | A leg's `trajectory` is computed when the plan is made (XFER-02) and stored on the leg. Rebuilding every trajectory from its order (ship, destination, arrival mode, `depart_day`, `travel_days`) with the same content gives the same trajectory, so a save holds orders and ships' states, not positions. | ✓ |
| PLAN-10 | **Transitions.** Scheduled → InFlight on `depart_day`; InFlight → Done on the arrival day (Rendezvous) or the crossing end day (Intercept, after which the ship is coasting). A Hold leg (PLAN-13) is Done at once. | ✓ |
| PLAN-11 | On `depart_day` the leg is re-validated: the ship must be in the departure orbit the plan assumed and have at least the leg's delta-v available (PLAN-21), and a target ship must still be parked in the orbit and at the phase the leg was solved against. If not, the plan is Failed and the ship is unchanged. | |
| PLAN-12 | A plan's last leg records, for display, the ship's **origin place**, **destination** and **arrival mode**, so a coasting ship's place can be described from its plan ("intercepted Halden IV, from Iver"). Text is the view's. | ✓ |
| PLAN-13 | **Hold** as a destination: the ship's current segment is extended without end and all later burns are dropped. For a parked ship it does nothing. An InFlight plan that is held stays on the ship as Holding, with its rail cut at that moment, so PLAN-12's origin, destination and arrival mode survive. | ✓ |
| PLAN-20 | A ship holds `delta_v_kms`, `reserved_kms` and `spent_kms`. A drive holds `impulsive`, `exhaust_velocity_kms` and `accel` (loaded acceleration, stored in km/day², set from the catalogue's m/s² by one conversion). | ✓ |
| PLAN-21 | **Available** delta-v = `delta_v_kms` − `reserved_kms` − `spent_kms`. Scheduling a plan reserves the leg's delta-v. A burn moves its amount from reserved to spent on the day it is made: departure cost + burn 1 at the crossing start, burn 2 + capture cost at the crossing end (Rendezvous only); a spiral moves its whole delta-v at the crossing start. Cancelling or failing a plan releases what it reserved. | ✓ |
| PLAN-22 | Cancelling a Scheduled plan leaves the ship as it was. Cancelling an InFlight plan is not specced (re-tasking). | ✓ |
| PLAN-30 | Per body record: the derived Low and High band radii (BAND-01). Per gate: `entry_tolerance_kms`, stored but not yet read by any rule. | ✓ |
